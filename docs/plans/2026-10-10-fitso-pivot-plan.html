Verdict

Pivot fitso.me into an AI stylist that makes three decisions for a person: what is working for them, what their wardrobe should be, and whether to buy a specific item. Sell a free quick win, a one-time Style Report, and an annual Pro plan. Grow only through content and shared result cards.

The real goal is USD 5,000 a month by December 2027, from this product or a family of products that grow out of it. This six-month plan is phase one: get as close as the funnel allows, steer every month, and learn which pillar compounds. The base case lands near 5,000 only if traffic ramps as modelled. The low case is about 1,900. Both are progress, and both are reviewed at the monthly check-in in section 13.

USD 5,000 a month needs about 125 paying buyers a month at a blended USD 35, plus about USD 700 in affiliate commissions. At the modelled funnel that needs about 15,000 visitors a month. The site has 51 today. Traffic is the binding constraint, not the product. Roughly 60 percent of the six-month effort goes to the content engine, 40 percent to the product.

Pricing is built to win the impulse: a real founding price, a real personal timer, a credit you earn by sharing, and nothing fake. Section 4.1 has the mechanism and section 14 the game theory behind it. Decisions that need your call are in section 12.

What the market says

Verified by a three-reviewer research pass unless marked. Nobody verified competes on honesty plus a buy verdict against a target wardrobe.

  • Whering is the best-funded direct competitor: about USD 7M seed led by eBay Ventures and Google AI Futures Fund, nearly USD 14M total, about 10M users, mostly Gen Z. Its AI features (virtual try-on, a styling chatbot) are announced, not shipped.
  • Alta raised USD 11M seed in June 2025 (Menlo Ventures) for an AI stylist and personal shopper. Free today, paid tiers undisclosed. Unverified beyond the funding.
  • Style DNA is the closest working product: selfie colour analysis, body-type guidance, a photo check of store items. Subscription only, with "poor value for money" and "unauthorized charges" among its top review complaints. Unverified, from app-store aggregators.
  • Colour analysis apps (My Best Colors, Drape, Colorayz, Dressika) mostly sell a one-time unlock. A few push weekly subscriptions. Unverified.
  • Myntra expected 200M annual active users by end of 2025, hit an 80M monthly peak in the festive season, and reports Gen Z as half its active base.
  • India online fashion is forecast to add about USD 56B at 21 percent a year from 2025 to 2030 (Technavio).
  • Conversion benchmark: hard-paywall mobile apps convert about 12 percent of installs to paid by day 35 against about 2 percent for open freemium (RevenueCat 2025 guest commentary, low confidence, mobile not web). Lesson: keep the free tier narrow and put the paywall right after the first visible result.
  • Payments: Dodo Payments is a merchant of record with India domestic cards and UPI at about 4 percent plus a fixed fee, plus 1.5 percent international and 0.5 percent on subscriptions. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy list 5 percent plus 50 cents, with Paddle's Indian onboarding reported as selective. Razorpay is 2 percent domestic but is not a merchant of record. Vendor pages only.
  • Affiliate: Myntra pays about 5 to 8 percent through networks and 8 to 12 percent through its creator program. Amazon India fashion pays about 5 to 9 percent. Affiliate blogs only.
  • Search demand for colour analysis, capsule wardrobes, and occasion outfits could not be quantified. The qualitative signal is strong: colour analysis is a sustained TikTok and Pinterest trend, and every 2026 menswear source frames the year as "fewer, better pieces".
3

The product: three decisions

01what is working for me
Style profile from photos. Colour season, face and feature read, body proportion, style archetype. Output leads with "three things working for you", then the rules that follow. This is the proof moment and the share hook.
02what should my wardrobe be
Target wardrobe plan. The user declares the wardrobe they want: life, climate, work, occasions, budget, vibe. The model builds a core wardrobe template, compares it to what they own, and produces a gap list and a phase-out list. This is what turns decision 03 into a real decision instead of "it suits you".
03should I buy this
Verdict on an item. Screenshot or link in. Out: yes, no, or yes-if, with reasons tied to the profile and the target. Every yes adds the item to the wardrobe. Every no is a saved purchase shown on the dashboard. This is the daily habit and the affiliate channel.

Honest flattery, as a product rule

1. Positives firstThe first screen of any result is "what is working for you", with at least three observations the model can point to in the photo.
2. Gaps are upgrades"A spread collar will do more for your jawline", never "avoid narrow collars".
3. No fake comparisonsNo "better than 80 percent of people". A percentile is a comparison the model cannot make and reads as a trick the second time.
4. Confidence shownA low-confidence colour read says "retake in daylight for a sharper call" instead of guessing.
5. The voiceA friend who dresses well and likes you. Not a fashion editor.

The wardrobe fills itself

Manual entry killed the first version. Three automatic inputs replace it.

Capture quality and privacy

Colour season reads are only as good as the lighting. The intake guides the user: daylight, no filter, neutral background, hair back, one close face shot, one full-length shot. The model returns a confidence score. Below the threshold the result says so and asks for a retake. Honest beats wrong.

Original photos are deleted after the profile is generated. Only the derived profile and a user-approved thumbnail are kept. This is both the right thing and a selling line.

4.1 Honest urgency: how the price wins the impulse

A visitor who sees USD 24 and no reason to decide today will decide tomorrow, and tomorrow never comes. Three mechanisms give a real reason to decide now. Each is true, each is enforced, and none runs on the landing page for a stranger who has done nothing yet.

MechanismWhat the user seesWhat makes it trueWhat it is for
Founding price"Founding price: Report INR 499 / USD 10, Pro INR 1,999 / USD 39, until fitso has earned its first USD 1,000. USD 612 of 1,000 so far."The counter is live from the payments webhook. At 1,000 the founding price ends and never returns. Founding Pro members keep their price on every renewal.scarcity that is public and verifiable. During the founding period the finish-today price is the founding price, so there is one number on the screen, not two.

1. Timers never resetNever extend, never reappear. Expired means expired. The expiry line on return is what makes the next timer believable.
2. Real numbersThe counter and the list price are real. The list price is what people pay after founding, and the base case is built on it.
3. Nothing for strangersNo countdown for anonymous visitors. Urgency starts after the user does something.
4. No fake capsNo "only N left" unless N is a real cap. Founding is capped by revenue, not a made-up seat count.
5. Refund brakeRefunds over 3 percent of sales in a month pause every urgency mechanism until the cause is found. The monthly review checks this.

Effect on the model: months one and two run at about USD 15 blended revenue per buyer instead of 34, and the first USD 1,000 should land in month two or early three. Conversion is expected to run higher under founding pricing, so the monthly buyer targets stand and the revenue targets for months one and two are soft. From month three the list price and the finish-today price carry the model.

The content engine

The one rule. Every piece of content is a question the product answers better than the page does. The page gives the generic answer in full. The call to action gives the personal one.

"Here are three Diwali looks for men. Want to know which one you can already wear from your closet, and what one item would upgrade it?" Content that does not map to a decision does not get made. No fashion news, no trend reports, no celebrity coverage.

Production and quality

  • AI drafts, you edit. A pillar template plus structured inputs (occasion, gender, region, budget, climate, local brands with prices) drafts a page. You review in batches.
  • Capacity: 10 pages a production day, two production days a week, about 80 pages a month.

  • Cadence: 2 production days a week on pages, 3 short videos a week, 20 pins a week, 3 Reddit replies a week.

Brand principles

Design direction, typefaces, and the full visual system are a separate pass in month one, built in pen.dev. This plan sets the principles only.

Lively, loud, colourfulColour is literally the product. The seasonal palettes are the brand's palette. A result card should be recognisable from across a room and look nothing like a wardrobe-management tool.
The result card is the logoMost people will meet the brand as a screenshot in someone's story. Design the card before the landing page.
Flattery with receiptsEvery compliment points at something in the photo. The voice is a friend who dresses well and is glad to see you.
Two doors, one houseThe men's and women's doors share a wordmark and a colour system but have their own layouts, photography style, and copy.

The name stays fitso. Tagline candidate: "Know what's working for you."

Game theory and gamification

A repeated game between fitso and the user, and a one-shot game with every stranger from search. The job is to make honesty the winning strategy and progress visible.

MechanismThe problem it solvesHow it shows up
Costly "no"A stylist who always says yes is cheap talk. The user cannot tell praise from sales.The verdict engine returns "no" or "yes-if" at least a third of the time across all users. The dashboard shows the user their own no's and the rupees saved. The no's are the proof that the yes's mean something. The monthly review flags a yes rate above 75 percent.
Incentive compatibility with affiliateA shopping assistant that earns on purchases is suspected of pushing them.The verdict is computed and shown before any link lookup. Links appear only on a yes, and the page says so. The rule is written on the pricing page.
Commitment devicePeople buy on impulse what they would not buy on reflection.The declared target wardrobe is the user's own rule. Every verdict is judged against it. The no-buy streak counts days since the last purchase made without a verdict. The user binds their future self, and fitso enforces.
Costly signallingSocial proof that is cheap to fake is ignored.A shared card reveals a model's read of your own face and style. That is a real disclosure, so it is credible. Because the card leads with strengths, sharing is cheap for the user and the signal is strong.
ReciprocityReferral programs feel like spam.A shared card gives the friend one free verdict and the sharer Pro credit. Both sides gain, nobody is asked to recruit.
Common knowledgePrivate deadlines do not move people.The founding counter is public. Everyone sees the same number and knows everyone sees it. That is what produces a rush.
Price discrimination by decision timeOne price loses either the impulse buyer or the deliberator.The finish-today price captures the impulse buyer. The list price captures the deliberator who returns. Nobody is lied to about which one they are.

Gamification, personal progress only

The product never ranks people against each other on looks, body, or face. Every game element is the user against their own target.

Wardrobe score, 0 to 100Coverage of the target wardrobe by what the user owns. The big number on the dashboard. Moves with every yes, import, and phase-out.
Gap list as a quest boardEach gap is a card: what it is, why it matters for the target, "find it", "I own this".
Saved purchasesA running rupee total of every no the user took. Loss aversion made visible, in the user's favour.
No-buy streakDays since the last purchase made without a verdict. Breaking it is not shamed. It resets.
Season passEach seasonal re-profile unlocks a new palette card. Four a year, collectible, shareable.
Badges tied to decisions"Profile complete", "Closed the gap", "Phase-out done", "Ten honest no's". Nothing for logging in.
Verdict feedback loop"I bought it" or "I returned it" earns a free verdict and feeds the accuracy data that content pages publish.
Not allowedPublic leaderboards, face or body scores, streak shaming, fake scarcity, notifications designed to make the user feel behind.
